Screen the frames

The scan recommends only what the code is genuinely sure of: blanks.
Blur is not judged here and never will be — across 15 focus measures the best global blur threshold reaches F1 = 0.37, and the textbook autofocus metric (variance of Laplacian) scores worse than chance: it is dominated by sensor noise, which is identical in sharp and blurry frames. Catching all 15 of the blurry frames in this dataset would also throw away 62 good ones. You meet every tile again in the sweep — exclude a blurry one there, with E, by eye. Nothing is auto-excluded. Tick what you want gone.
Two different questions, and the code answers neither. Exclude throws the frame out of the mosaic entirely — that is yours to tick. Let the matcher score it lifts the correlator's refusal: by default a frame the measure calls blank is refused, not scored, because two blank frames 136 trials apart correlate +0.43 at zero shift — what they share is fixed-pattern sensor structure, which does not move with the stage, so they register confidently and wrongly. But this measure has no margin at the boundary and the usable and blank frames interleave below it, so some of these are ordinary tiles: on this dataset three of the four land sub-pixel on the truth once scored, and the fourth is 679 px wrong at NCC 0.66. Look at the thumbnail. You decide, per frame. (A refused frame is not lost: you can always drop it in by hand in the sweep.)
